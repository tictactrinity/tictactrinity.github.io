# [Refutations](/refutations/) > [Mercy and Love](/refutations/mercy_and_love/) > Selective
Author: Fasih Rana  
Dated: 27-September-2026

## Introduction
[John 1:14](https://biblehub.com/john/1-14.htm) says _the Word became flesh_, naming the incarnation as specifically the **Word**, not the Father or the Spirit. Is the divine essence genuinely undivided?

## Premises
**P1**. Trinitarian doctrine holds there is one, undivided divine essence, fully shared by whatever is called Father, Son, and Spirit.  
**P2**. Whatever distinguishes the Son from the Father and Spirit is either (i) some content that belongs to the essence, or (ii) nothing belonging to the essence at all making it a bare label with no content of its own.  
**P3**. [John 1:14](https://biblehub.com/john/1-14.htm) attributes the incarnation specifically to the Word/Son and not to the Father or Spirit.  
**P4**. _The Word was God_ ([John 1:1](https://biblehub.com/john/1-1.htm)) is a substantive, descriptive claim about what the **Word** is, not a bare, contentless label.

## Conclusion
If (i), the distinguishing content belongs to the undivided essence (P1), and so belongs equally to the Father and Spirit, meaning the incarnation should apply to them too, contradicting P3.  
If (ii), **Word** carries no content beyond an empty label; contradicting P4, which shows the term is describing something real about what the Word is.  
∴ Either option contradicts one of the doctrine's own claims: the exclusivity of the incarnation (P3) or the descriptive force of _the Word was God_ (P4).
