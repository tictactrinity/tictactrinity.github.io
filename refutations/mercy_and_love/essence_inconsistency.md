# [Refutations](/refutations/) > [Mercy and Love](/refutations/mercy_and_love/) > Essence Inconsistency

Author: Fasih Rana  
Dated: 27-September-2026

## Introduction
Trinitarians have a view that the Son is the _Word_ (or speech) of God and that it is a separate person within the godhead. But is it consistent?

## Premises
**P1**. Trinitarian doctrine holds that all three persons share one, single, undivided divine essence. No person possesses more, less, or a different portion of it than another.  
**P2**. "God is love" ([1 John 4:8](https://biblehub.com/1_john/4-8.htm)) is treated as true of the whole divine essence, predicated equally of Father, Son, and Spirit alike.  
**P3**. "The Word" ([John 1:1](https://biblehub.com/john/1-1.htm)) is used to name the Son specifically, as his distinguishing personal property, not predicated equally of all three persons.  
**P4**. If **love** and **word** are both essence-terms, they should both apply equally to all persons under P1 **or** _neither_ can be used to distinguish one person from the others.  

## Conclusion
Therefore, treating **Word** as the Son's distinguishing mark, while treating **Love** as common to all three persons, is inconsistent with P1's claim of one shared, undivided essence; the same category of term (an essence-descriptor) is being used to do two incompatible jobs (universal attribute vs. personal distinguishing name) with no principled rule for which is which.
