# [Refutations](/refutations/) > [Mercy and Love](/refutations/mercy_and_love/) > Love is not a Person?

Author: Fasih Rana  
Dated: 27-September-2026

## Introduction
Trinitarians have a view that the Son is the _Word_ (or speech) of God and that it is a separate person within the godhead. But is it consistent?

## Premises
**P1**. Trinitarians hold that the Son is the Word (Logos) of God, who became flesh as Jesus ([John 1:1](https://biblehub.com/john/1-1.htm), [John 1:14](https://biblehub.com/john/1-14.htm)).  
**P2**. **"Word"** functions in John's Gospel as a description of what God is; a mode of God's own essence, not something separate from God.  
**P3**. Other terms in the New Testament describe God's essence the same way; e.g., "God is **love**" ([1 John 4:8](https://biblehub.com/1_john/4-8.htm)), "God is **light**" ([1 John 1:5](https://biblehub.com/1_john/1-5.htm)), "God is **spirit**" ([John 4:24](https://biblehub.com/john/4-24.htm)).  
**P4**. Trinitarian doctrine treats only one of these essence-descriptions ("Word") as corresponding to a distinct divine person, while treating the others ("love," "light," "spirit") as mere attributes with no corresponding person.  
**P5**. There is no principled reason given, from the grammar of these texts themselves, why "Word" earns personhood while "love" and "light" do not while they are all are stated using the same **"God is X"** construction.

## Conclusion
Therefore the move from **Word** to _a distinct person of the Trinity_ is arbitrary: it isn't demanded by the text's grammar (which treats these terms alike). So either every essence-term should generate a person; making the Godhead an unknown, unbounded number of persons; or none should, in which case the Son's personhood via **the Word** has no textual grounding either.
