# [Refutations](/refutations/) > John 5 - Does Jesus Judge on his own?

**Author: Fasih Rana, Date: 19-Aug-2026**

# Intro

John 5 is often presented as a (partial) proof for Jesus's and one of the arguments put forth is that Jesus is the sole Judge. [John 5:22](https://biblehub.com/john/5-22.htm) is one of the most cited verses for it.

> For the Father judges no one, but has committed all judgment to the Son,

This, Christians say, is the proof that Jesus is _God_ and he will come back to judge all of humanity, but lets test this.

# Jesus is Dependent
## Premises

**P1.** Jesus always tells the truth.  
**P2.** Jesus says in 5:19, the Son can do nothing of himself, but only what he sees the Father doing.  
**P3.** Jesus says in 5:22, the Father judges no one.  
**P4.** Jesus says in 5:30, the Son judges as he hears.  

## Conclusions

**C1.** If P3 means the Father is entirely uninvolved in judgment, then P4 is false — you cannot hear guidance from someone with no involvement.  
**C2.** If C1 is true, P2 is also false. The Son would be acting entirely independently, doing something of himself.  
**C3.** If C1 is true, Jesus contradicts himself three times within the same passage.  
**C4.** If C3 is true, P1 is false which would mean Jesus is a liar.  

# Jesus is not Almighty
These premises were formulated based on the debate of [Shiekh Uthman vs 10 Christian Apologists](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HMfA-FtYWMI&t=3825s). The initial prompt was **Jesus is not Almighty God according to the Bible**. Shiekh Uthman is talking to Jerome Chukwuwem, who initialliy responds by asking _"if Jesus claimed to be almighty, would he then be Almighty God?"_. Later on Jerome makes a statement that _"the author of John believes that Jesus is equal to the Father"_. This goes away from from the initial prompt but lets formulate some premises anyway.

## Premises
**P1.** The author of John presents the Father and Son as equal in John 5:18.  
**P2.** Trinitarian doctrine holds that the Father and Son share an identical divine nature.  
**P3.** Divine nature necessarily entails Almighty Nature.  
**P4.** Almighty nature necessarily entails full, independent authority to initiate action which is not derived, received or contingent on another.

## Jerome's Conclusion
**Q.** If Jesus claimed to be almighty, he would be claiming to be God.

## Conclusions
**C1.** By Jesus's own admission, he does not possess the power/ability to act without the Father (5:19), and he judges only as he hears (5:30).  
**C2.** Jesus states in 5:22 that the Father gave judgment to the Son — meaning the Son did not already possess it independently.  
**C3.** Furthermore, 5:20 and 5:30 show this is an ongoing relationship of dependence, not a one-time transfer. By Jesus's own repeated admission, dependence is the present, continuing mode of his action, not a past event.  
**C4.** By P4, almighty nature necessarily entails authority not derived from another. C1-C3 show the opposite; authority and judgements that are being received continuously, not self-possessed. Therefore the Son does not exhibit the property P4 requires for almightiness.  
**C5.** By Q, since Jesus does not exhibit the independent almighty authority P2–P4 require, the claim to equality (P1) is not a claim to being Almighty God.  
**C6.** If it is instead claimed that the Son merely suspends his independent authority to judge (rather than never possessing it), then that authority cannot be an essential property of almightiness in the first place, since P4 defines almighty nature as necessarily entailing independent authority, and a truly necessary property cannot be suspended without the nature itself ceasing to be what P4 defines it as. The suspension defense therefore contradicts P4, rather than rescuing the conclusion.
